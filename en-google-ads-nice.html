On a small budget, one rule

Every euro must be traceable to a specific search you can read and understand.

No "let Google optimise it".

When the budget is tight there's no volume to hide behind. A tenth of it going to the wrong place shows up immediately — provided somebody looks.

How I work

I start by checking people can reach you

Before spending a euro for you. On the account above, the site's contact button had been broken from day one — the client was paying for visitors who couldn't call him. None of it showed anywhere inside Google.

I pick few searches, very precise ones

Rather than casting wide and hoping. You can read the list of searches you're paying for and tell me yourself which are worthless for your trade — you know your customers better than I do.

I keep control of the bidding

Google's automatic settings need a lot of volume to be good. On a small local budget they cost money while they learn. I'd rather steer by hand until there's enough volume to justify otherwise.

I tell you what the numbers don't prove

It's what sets me apart most, and what loses me the most work. In the published case study I fixed a serious fault and then wrote plainly that I could not demonstrate my fix had improved the results. The data didn't allow it.

What to know before starting

  • Don't cut your budget. On that account, stopping and restarting pushed the price per visitor up 30%. Better €5 a day continuously than €150 one week in three.
  • Advertising stops when the payments stop. The day you switch off, you disappear. That's the major difference from SEO, which takes months but stays in place.
  • Someone has to answer. If nobody picks up between noon and two, part of that budget is wasted. We set the hours around your reality, not the theory.
