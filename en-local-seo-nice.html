What I actually do

In this order, because it's the order that pays back fastest.

1 · Your Google listing

Exact categories, service area, hours, photos, description, services listed one by one. Most listings I look at are half filled in — and the missing half is what decides whether you appear on the map.

2 · Your reviews

How many reviews you have matters as much as the score. We set up a simple way to ask every satisfied customer, without it costing you time, and we reply to the ones that come in — including the bad ones.

3 · One page per service, one per town

A single "our services" page ranks for nothing. A page per job and per town you cover, written to be read by a human, is what gets you found on specific searches.

4 · The site itself

Loading speed on mobile, headings, structure, a tappable phone number. Nothing spectacular — but a site that's slow or unreadable on a phone loses customers before it ever has them.

How long it takes — the real answer

SEO isn't a switch. Here's an honest order of magnitude, not a promise:

  • Weeks 1 to 4 — the Google listing and reviews. This moves fastest, because it's what most businesses have done worst.
  • Months 2 to 4 — the pages on your site. Google takes time to find them, then to decide whether they deserve showing.
  • Beyond that — it's maintenance. Reviews, new pages, competitors moving.

If someone promises you first place in three weeks, they're selling you either disguised advertising or nothing at all. I'd rather lose a client at this point than after three months.

SEO or advertising? They answer different needs

Google advertising

You appear at the top tomorrow. You pay per visitor. Stop paying and you vanish the same day.

Useful when: you need calls now, or you want to test whether a service interests people before investing.

SEO

You won't appear for several months. After that, each call costs you nothing. And it doesn't stop if you skip a month's budget.

Useful when: you're building for the long run and you're tired of paying for every customer who finds you.

In practice many businesses run a small advertising budget while the SEO builds, then cut the advertising back once the free calls arrive. It isn't compulsory — just what works best when cash is tight.

Questions I get asked

Do you guarantee first position?

No, and be wary of anyone who does. Google doesn't sell places in the natural results. What I can do is fix what's stopping you appearing, and show you what moves.

Do I need a new website?

Usually not. We start with the Google listing, the reviews and the pages you already have. Rebuilding only makes sense if the site is genuinely unusable — and then I'll tell you, with reasons.

What does it cost?

It depends on your trade and how many towns you're targeting, so I can't put an honest price on a web page. The free diagnosis gives me enough to quote you a precise figure, and you can say no.

Does it work for my trade?

If your customers find you by searching Google, yes. If you live purely on word of mouth and tenders, your money is honestly better spent elsewhere — and I'll say so rather than take the budget.
